# World Canon: Locations

> Canon tier legend: **[fixed]** never changed by agents · **[current]** changed only by
> engine events, tracked in `state/` · **[flavor]** agents may extend in-voice.
>
> Each entry gives the engine slug, the district or realm it belongs to, comics-canon
> history (Earth-616, classic/most enduring version where retconned), the current state at
> launch, who frequents it, the landmarks a tilemap artist needs, and arc hooks. Travel
> times between these locations live in `rules.md`. "Sub-map" means an interior or hidden
> area that loads as its own tilemap from a door or trigger tile on the parent map.

## midtown-manhattan — Midtown Manhattan

**District:** Manhattan, roughly 34th to 59th Street, river to river. The hub map; most
other Manhattan launch locations are buildings on it or adjacent to it.

**History [fixed].** Midtown is the Marvel Universe's most contested square mile. Every
major hero has fought here; every major villain has made a point of fighting here because
the cameras are here. The Baxter Building, the Daily Bugle, Stark/Avengers Tower, Oscorp
and Fisk's holdings are all within swinging distance of one another, which is why street
fights so often become team-ups. Spider-Man calls the rooftops his "highway" and knows the
water towers by name. Grand Central and Times Square have been smashed and rebuilt so often
that the city keeps a standing contract with Damage Control, Inc., the Marvel Universe's
superhuman-cleanup firm.

**Current state [current].** Fully rebuilt as of launch. Scaffolding on one Times Square
façade from an unnamed prior incident (rebuild timer: 3 game-days). Doctor Octopus's lair
sits under a condemned transformer station on the West Side waterfront, undiscovered.

**Who frequents it.** Everyone. Peter Parker (Bugle errands, ESU adjacent), Tony Stark,
the Avengers on patrol, J. Jonah Jameson, Norman Osborn, Wilson Fisk's lieutenants, NYPD
Midtown South, tourists, hot-dog vendors, street-level crooks drawn from the incident table.

**Landmarks for the tilemap.** Times Square screens (hijackable; palette-shift at night),
Grand Central façade and clock, Empire State Building (tall sprite, climbable), Rockefeller
Center plaza, Fifth Avenue storefronts, Midtown South precinct, a hospital (injury
destination), subway entrances, yellow cabs, scaffolding tiles, water towers and rooftop
HVAC (Spider-Man's traversal layer), the condemned transformer station (door tile to Ock's
lair sub-map), construction cranes (throwable/destructible T2).

**Arc hooks.** Doctor Octopus's reactor scheme (Geiger tells near the waterfront); Red
Skull's broadcast hijack of the Times Square screens; any villain who wants an audience;
collateral-damage pressure on Public Opinion; the Bugle, Oscorp and Fisk Tower as
walk-to destinations in a single scene.

## hells-kitchen — Hell's Kitchen

**District:** Manhattan West Side, 34th to 59th Street, Eighth Avenue to the Hudson.

**History [fixed].** Matt Murdock's neighborhood. He grew up here, the son of "Battlin'"
Jack Murdock, a washed-up boxer murdered by the Fixer's men for refusing to throw a fight.
Matt lost his sight saving a blind man from a truck carrying radioactive waste; the same
accident gave him his radar sense. He trains at Fogwell's Gym where his father trained,
drinks at Josie's Bar where half the clientele are informants, and practices law with Foggy
Nelson out of a storefront office. The Kitchen has been the front line of Daredevil's war
with the Kingpin for decades; Fisk owns buildings here, judges here, and police here.
Elektra Natchios and the Hand have used its rooftops; Bullseye has killed on them.

**Current state [current].** Kingpin's grip is firm but quiet. Nelson & Murdock is open.
Red Skull's Hydra cell occupies a pre-war bank vault beneath a shuttered theater on Tenth
Avenue, unknown to Fisk and to Daredevil. Daredevil's informant network is intact.

**Who frequents it.** Matt Murdock / Daredevil, Foggy Nelson, Josie, Fisk's enforcers,
Ben Urich (reporting), Spider-Man (shared beat), Hydra operatives in civilian clothes,
Sister Maggie at Clinton Church.

**Landmarks for the tilemap.** Tenement rooftops with fire escapes and clotheslines
(Daredevil's traversal layer), Fogwell's Gym (neon sign, door to interior sub-map),
Josie's Bar (door tile; dialogue hub), Nelson & Murdock office (ground-floor storefront),
Clinton Church spire, Matt's brownstone, Hudson piers and warehouses, Lincoln Tunnel
approach, the shuttered theater (door to Hydra vault sub-map), alleys with dumpsters.

**Arc hooks.** Red Skull's war on Fisk (Streets of Poison redux) with the Kitchen as the
front; Kingpin's pressure on Nelson & Murdock; Daredevil learning who is really under the
theater; Spider-Man and Daredevil's trusted-ally scenes; Ben Urich stories.

## forest-hills — Forest Hills, Queens

**District:** Queens. Residential, Tudor-style houses, quiet streets, a long subway ride
from Midtown.

**History [fixed].** Peter Parker's childhood home at 20 Ingram Street, where Ben and May
Parker raised him and where Uncle Ben was shot by a burglar Peter had let escape (Amazing
Fantasy #15). Anna Watson, Mary Jane's aunt, lives next door; it was May and Anna who kept
trying to set Peter up with "Anna's niece." Midtown High School, where Peter was bullied
by Flash Thompson and first saw the radioactive-spider demonstration's aftermath, is here.
Doctor Octopus boarded in May's house (ASM #53-56) and nearly married her (ASM #130-131).
Uncle Ben is buried in a nearby cemetery that Peter visits when he has failed someone.

**Current state [current].** May lives alone in the Ingram Street house. Peter visits less
than he should. The house is in modest repair; the porch step creaks. No villain has openly
attacked the neighborhood in this continuity, which is why May feels safe.

**Who frequents it.** Aunt May, Anna Watson, Peter Parker (guilty visits), Mary Jane (with
Peter), Flash Thompson, neighbors drawn from the schedule table; Doctor Octopus, in a hat
and coat, if his "Mrs. Parker" scheme advances.

**Landmarks for the tilemap.** 20 Ingram Street (Parker house, door to interior sub-map:
kitchen, Peter's old attic room), Anna Watson's house next door, Midtown High School
façade and athletic field, a pharmacy and corner grocery, the cemetery (Ben's headstone
is an interact tile), the elevated subway station, tree-lined streets with parked cars.

**Arc hooks.** Aunt May's health; "the gentleman caller"; whether May learns Peter's secret
(Showrunner-planned only, Editor approval); any villain who has learned Peter's identity
turning up on the porch; the quiet domestic scene after a loss.

## avengers-tower — Avengers Tower

**District:** Midtown Manhattan. Formerly Stark Tower. **In this canon `stark-tower` and
`avengers-tower` are the same building**; `stark-tower` is an alias slug that resolves to
this entry.

**History [fixed].** Tony Stark built Stark Tower as the Manhattan headquarters of Stark
Enterprises, a gleaming spire with a landing pad and a corporate lobby. When Avengers
Mansion on Fifth Avenue was destroyed during the Scarlet Witch's breakdown (Avengers
Disassembled, Avengers #500-503) and the team dissolved, Stark offered the tower's upper
floors to the re-formed Avengers (New Avengers #1-6). It has been attacked, infiltrated,
dropped on, and in one era topped by the Sentry's Watchtower. Edwin Jarvis runs the
household. The old mansion site remains a memorial.

**Current state [current].** Operational Avengers headquarters. Quinjet hangar stocked
with two craft. Medbay staffed. Tony's armory holds his current armor and two prior marks.
Jarvis is in residence; Pepper Potts keeps an office on the corporate floors.

**Who frequents it.** Captain America, Iron Man, Thor, Hulk (when he is Banner and
welcome), Spider-Man (nervously), Pepper Potts, Jarvis, Nick Fury (unannounced), Jane
Foster (visiting Thor).

**Landmarks for the tilemap.** Exterior: a tall, distinctive spire with the stylized "A"
(a T3 destructible landmark), landing pad on the roof, glass lobby, street-level plaza.
Interior sub-maps: lobby and elevator, assembly room with the round table and the big
screen, Quinjet hangar, Tony's workshop and armory (armor-repair tile), medbay (healing
modifier), Jarvis's kitchen, living quarters, a balcony for brooding.

**Arc hooks.** Team briefings that open arcs; Fury dropping intel on Hydra; Tony's armor
states as a gear resource; the tower as a target villains attack to make a statement; the
Hulk question (is Banner welcome this week); Spider-Man feeling out of place.

## baxter-building — The Baxter Building

**District:** Midtown Manhattan, 42nd Street and Madison Avenue.

**History [fixed].** Headquarters of the Fantastic Four, who lease its top five floors.
Reed Richards' laboratories, the portal to the Negative Zone, a rooftop hangar for the
Fantasticar, and a long-suffering landlord are its canon fixtures. Doctor Doom once tore
the entire building out of the ground and hauled it into space (Fantastic Four #6); Kristoff
Vernard, programmed to believe he was Doom, later destroyed it outright, and the team
rebuilt on the site as Four Freedoms Plaza before the Baxter Building name returned. The
building is the Marvel Universe's most frequently stolen piece of real estate.

**Current state [current].** The Fantastic Four are off-world and the building is sealed
under Reed's automated security. The Negative Zone portal is dormant and locked. The
lobby's robotic receptionist still takes messages.

**Who frequents it.** At launch, nobody inside. Outside: tourists, the occasional hero
checking whether the FF are back, villains testing the security.

**Landmarks for the tilemap.** A square mid-century tower with a big "4" (T3 destructible
landmark), rooftop hangar doors, street-level lobby door (locked at launch), the famous
giant hole in the ground if a Showrunner ever lets Doom repeat FF #6. Interior sub-map is
Phase 2: Reed's lab, the Negative Zone portal chamber.

**Arc hooks.** Doom's fixation on Reed is a reason for Doom to be in Manhattan; a villain
trying to open the Negative Zone portal (Phase 2 realm); the FF's return as a roster event;
Spider-Man and the Human Torch's rivalry whenever the Torch is in town.

## sanctum-sanctorum — The Sanctum Sanctorum

**District:** Greenwich Village, 177A Bleecker Street.

**History [fixed].** Home of Doctor Stephen Strange, Sorcerer Supreme, and his friend and
steward Wong. A three-story Victorian townhouse built on a site of ancient power; it has
stood on ley lines since before the city existed. The round window on its top floor bears
the Seal of the Vishanti, which both wards the house and marks it as a target. The interior
is larger than the exterior, rooms change position, and the library holds books that
should not exist. It has been invaded by Dormammu's forces, Baron Mordo, the Hood's demons
and worse, and it is still standing because Strange is very good and Wong is better than
he looks.

**Current state [current].** Strange is away (Phase 2 roster). Wong keeps house and does
not open the door to strangers. The wards are up. A faint amber glow in the Seal window
at night.

**Who frequents it.** Wong. Occasionally a hero who needs a mystic question answered and
leaves with a cup of tea and no answer. Loki has reasons to be interested.

**Landmarks for the tilemap.** Narrow Village street with brownstones, the Sanctum's
distinctive façade and the round Seal window (glows; not destructible), iron fence, front
steps (door tile; locked unless Wong opens it). Interior sub-map is Phase 2: foyer with
grand staircase, library, meditation chamber, the room with the Orb of Agamotto.

**Arc hooks.** Loki testing the wards; an artifact Wong refuses to hand over; Strange's
return as the Phase 2 introduction; a mystic threat that the launch roster cannot punch.

## daily-bugle — The Daily Bugle

**District:** East Midtown Manhattan (Second Avenue by most references).

**History [fixed].** New York's loudest newspaper, founded in the nineteenth century and
owned since the 1960s by J. Jonah Jameson, who has spent the whole of Spider-Man's career
calling him a menace in 72-point type. Joe "Robbie" Robertson, the editor-in-chief, is the
paper's conscience. Betty Brant went from Jameson's secretary to a reporter. Ben Urich, the
Bugle's best investigative reporter, worked out Daredevil's identity and has kept it. Peter
Parker sells the Bugle photographs of Spider-Man, which is how the Bugle gets the best
pictures of the man it hates. The building has been attacked by the Green Goblin, the
Scorpion (whom Jameson created), and nearly every rogue who wanted a headline.

**Current state [current].** Jameson is publisher and running an anti-Spider-Man campaign.
Robertson is editor. Peter is owed money and owes photos. The rooftop sign is intact.

**Who frequents it.** J. Jonah Jameson, Robbie Robertson, Betty Brant, Ben Urich, Peter
Parker, Mary Jane (dropping by), news vendors, the occasional villain with a grievance.

**Landmarks for the tilemap.** Mid-rise office building with the big rooftop DAILY BUGLE
sign (T3 destructible landmark; a classic set-piece is somebody hanging from it), a
newsstand out front with the day's headline (interact tile shows the headline, which is a
Public Opinion tell), loading dock for delivery trucks. Interior sub-map: bullpen with
desks and typewriter-era clutter, Jameson's office (door slams), the photo lab, the roof.

**Arc hooks.** The Bugle's campaign as a Public Opinion lever; Jameson learning anything
about Peter; Ben Urich's investigations touching Fisk or Hydra; a villain seizing the
building to broadcast; Betty Brant's reporting; the headline tile as the world's daily
recap mechanism.

## oscorp-tower — Oscorp Tower

**District:** Midtown Manhattan.

**History [fixed].** Headquarters of Oscorp Industries, Norman Osborn's chemical and
technology conglomerate. Osborn built the company with his partner Mendel Stromm, forced
Stromm out, and in Oscorp's labs tested the unstable strength-enhancing formula that gave
him his powers and his madness; the Green Goblin's gliders, pumpkin bombs and gas were all
Oscorp engineering. Harry Osborn grew up in the shadow of the building and inherited both
the company and the legacy. Oscorp has been a respectable public company, a front, a
weapons supplier, and a crime scene, sometimes in the same month.

**Current state [current].** Norman Osborn is publicly a respected industrialist with a
troubled past. Oscorp operates normally. Hidden Goblin equipment caches exist within the
building; the Green Goblin's bible governs where and how many.

**Who frequents it.** Norman Osborn, Harry Osborn, Oscorp security and scientists, Peter
Parker (visiting Harry, uneasily), Doctor Octopus if the Osborn offer advances, corporate
visitors including Stark competitors.

**Landmarks for the tilemap.** A modern glass-and-steel tower with the Oscorp logo (T3
destructible), executive floor with a corner office overlooking the city, a rooftop glider
launch concealed as HVAC, loading docks. Interior sub-map: lobby, Norman's office (door to
hidden lair tile, Showrunner-gated), a chemistry lab.

**Arc hooks.** Norman's long game against Peter; Harry's stability; Oscorp contracts as a
tell for Ock's or Fisk's schemes; a lab accident that becomes a street incident; corporate
rivalry with Stark.

## fisk-tower — Fisk Tower

**District:** Midtown Manhattan.

**History [fixed].** The legitimate face of Wilson Fisk, the Kingpin of Crime: a holding
company headquarters from whose top floor he runs import-export, real estate, and the
largest criminal organization on the East Coast. Fisk's office is spare and enormous, with
a desk he can lift and a window he has thrown people through. The building has been raided
by the police and found spotless. Daredevil has fought his way up it and Spider-Man has
fought his way down it. Fisk's late wife Vanessa haunts the office in the form of a portrait
and a silence; his son Richard's betrayals are not spoken of. (The name "Fisk Tower" is this
world's label for the Kingpin's canonical Midtown headquarters.)

**Current state [current].** Fully operational. Fisk is in residence most days. The NYPD
has nothing on him. One of his lieutenants has been quietly asking about Hydra.

**Who frequents it.** Wilson Fisk, his bodyguards and lawyers, lieutenants, bribed
officials, Daredevil and Spider-Man uninvited, Ben Urich outside with a notebook.

**Landmarks for the tilemap.** A black-stone tower, broad and heavy like its owner (T3
destructible), a private garage entrance, a lobby with metal detectors. Interior sub-map:
Fisk's top-floor office (desk, portrait, the window), a boardroom, a private gym, a vault.

**Arc hooks.** Kingpin's war with Red Skull; Kingpin financing Doctor Octopus; pressure on
Nelson & Murdock; a Daredevil ascent set-piece; Fisk's standing with NYPD as a faction
dynamic.

## george-washington-bridge — The George Washington Bridge

**District:** Upper Manhattan / Hudson River, connecting Washington Heights to New Jersey.

**History [fixed].** Where Gwen Stacy died (Amazing Spider-Man #121, 1973). The Green
Goblin took her to the top of the bridge and threw her off; Spider-Man's webline caught her
ankle, and the snap of the sudden stop may have killed her. (The issue's text says
Brooklyn Bridge; the art and all subsequent canon say the George Washington Bridge.) It is
the single most painful place in Peter Parker's world. He does not swing past it if he can
route around it. Norman Osborn knows exactly what the bridge means to him.

**Current state [current].** Intact. Traffic normal. A small informal memorial bouquet
appears on the walkway some mornings; nobody has seen who leaves it.

**Who frequents it.** Commuters. Spider-Man, never by choice. The Green Goblin when he wants
to hurt Peter. The Showrunner should treat it as a reserved stage.

**Landmarks for the tilemap.** Two steel towers with cables (climbable; the top platform is
a boss-arena tile set), the roadway with traffic (hazard), pedestrian walkway, the river
far below (fall = Showrunner-planned only), the memorial tile, the Washington Heights
approach and the Little Red Lighthouse beneath the span.

**Arc hooks.** Goblin's taunts and the anniversary of Gwen's death; Harry Osborn confronting
the site; a hostage held where Gwen was held; Spider-Man forced to choose a route past it.
Any fall from this bridge is reserved for the Showrunner and the Editor.

## chelsea-rooftop — The Chelsea Water Tower

**District:** Chelsea, Manhattan, west of Midtown and south of Hell's Kitchen.

**History [fixed, world-authored].** Not a comics landmark; it is the "unremarkable water
tower in Chelsea where he eats and thinks" named in Spider-Man's bible. A tar-paper roof on
a six-story loft building, a wooden water tower on iron legs, a milk crate he dragged up
years ago, and a clear line of sight to the Empire State Building. He has eaten a hundred
cold dinners here, patched his costume here, and sat here after Gwen, after Captain Stacy,
and after every loss since. Mary Jane knows the address. Daredevil found it by listening.

**Current state [current].** Milk crate present. A half-eaten sandwich in a paper bag,
weighted with a brick, is roughly two game-days old.

**Who frequents it.** Spider-Man alone. Mary Jane when he does not come home. Daredevil
when he needs to talk. The Black Cat if she is in the city. Pigeons.

**Landmarks for the tilemap.** Water tower (climbable, sit-tile on the ledge), tar roof
with skylights, a rusted door to a stairwell, neighboring rooftops at different heights,
the Empire State Building in the distant parallax layer, a sunset/night palette that
matters more here than anywhere else.

**Arc hooks.** The quiet scene after a loss; a mask-off conversation with MJ; a villain who
has followed him here (the invasion of the one place that is his); the moment he decides
to stop hiding an injury.

## latveria-castle — Castle Doom, Latveria

**Realm/region:** Latveria, a small Balkan kingdom bordered by Symkaria, Hungary, Serbia
and Romania. The castle stands on a crag above the capital, Doomstadt.

**History [fixed].** Victor von Doom seized Latveria from its hereditary king and has ruled
it ever since as absolute monarch, benevolent in his own estimation and intolerable in
everyone else's. His father Werner, a Romani healer, died hiding Victor from the old
baron's soldiers; his mother Cynthia, a sorceress, lost her soul to Mephisto, and Doom's
yearly attempts to free it are the only thing he has ever failed at repeatedly and kept
trying. Castle Doom holds his laboratories, the Time Platform he built after his
expulsion from Empire State University, the Doombot foundry, and the dungeons. His faithful
retainer Boris has served the family since Victor was a boy. Kristoff Vernard, Doom's ward
and heir, was raised in these halls. Doom once hurled the Baxter Building into space from
here and has fought the Fantastic Four in the throne room more times than either side can
count. Latverians are well-fed, well-schooled and unfree, and most of them prefer it.

**Current state [current].** Doom rules. The castle is at full strength: Doombots on every
wall, the Time Platform locked, Boris in attendance. Doomstadt is quiet and watched. The
Latverian embassy in New York is active.

**Who frequents it.** Doctor Doom, Boris, Doombots, Kristoff Vernard, the Latverian Guard,
diplomats, prisoners, and heroes who arrive by Quinjet without an invitation.

**Landmarks for the tilemap.** Doomstadt: a medieval town square with a statue of Doom,
half-timbered houses, a Latverian Guard post, the funicular or switchback road up to the
castle. Castle exterior: grey stone walls, green banners, a drawbridge, Doombot sentries on
the ramparts. Castle interior sub-map (boss arena): great hall with throne, the Time
Platform chamber, the laboratory, the Doombot foundry (phase 1 arena), the throne room
(phase 2), a hidden balcony or chamber for the reveal (phase 3: "It was a Doombot").

**Arc hooks.** Doom's three-phase boss template from the design doc; Doom summoning a hero
to negotiate; a Latverian "diplomatic" presence in Manhattan; Doom's contempt for Red Skull
and Doctor Octopus as potential alliances or feuds; Loki's interest in Cynthia von Doom's
soul; the Latveria faction's standing with heroes who have invaded.

## asgard — Asgard, the Realm Eternal

**Realm:** One of the Nine Realms on the World Tree Yggdrasil, reached from Midgard
(Earth) by the Bifrost. **Editorial decision:** in this world Asgard is in its classic
mythic location across the Rainbow Bridge, not floating over Oklahoma or any later
arrangement.

**History [fixed].** Home of the Asgardians, ruled by Odin All-Father from the throne
Hlidskjalf in the golden city. Thor, Odin's son by the Earth goddess Gaea, was raised here
alongside the adopted Loki, son of the Frost Giant Laufey. Heimdall guards the Bifrost and
sees all who approach. The Warriors Three (Fandral, Hogun, Volstagg), Sif, Balder the Brave
and Frigga are the court. Loki has schemed against this throne since boyhood, from petty
mischief to Ragnarok itself, and has been forgiven more often than any being in the Nine
Realms. The realm cycles through Ragnarok and rebirth; this world is in a stable interval.
Odin's Vault beneath the palace holds the Destroyer armor, the Odinsword, and worse.

**Current state [current].** Odin reigns. Thor divides his time between Asgard and Midgard.
Loki is in disfavor but not imprisoned, which is the most dangerous arrangement. Heimdall
has been told to watch Loki's movements and has been doing so.

**Who frequents it.** Thor, Loki, Odin, Frigga, Heimdall, Sif, the Warriors Three, Balder,
Asgardian soldiers and citizens, Jane Foster if Thor brings her.

**Landmarks for the tilemap.** The Bifrost (a rainbow bridge tile set with Heimdall's
observatory at the end; arrival/departure point), the golden city's towers, Odin's palace
and the great hall with Hlidskjalf, the training yards, Loki's quarters (dark tower with
green accents), the Hall of Valhalla's doors, the vaults beneath the palace (locked), the
void at the realm's edge (fall hazard; Showrunner-only).

**Arc hooks.** Loki's schemes against Odin and Thor; the Destroyer or an Asgardian relic
loose on Midgard; Odin summoning Thor home at the worst moment; Heimdall refusing a hero
passage; Loki using Asgard's politics to escape Midgard consequences.

## xaviers-school — Xavier's School for Gifted Youngsters (Phase 2)

**Region:** 1407 Graymalkin Lane, Salem Center, Westchester County, New York, about an
hour north of Manhattan.

**History [fixed].** Charles Xavier's ancestral mansion and, since X-Men #1 (1963), the
school where he trains young mutants and houses the X-Men. The Danger Room, Cerebro, the
sub-basement laboratories and the hangar under the grounds are canon fixtures. The mansion
has been destroyed and rebuilt many times (Sentinels, Magneto, Bastion, the Phalanx) and is
always rebuilt on the same spot. Wolverine and Storm, Phase 2 roster members, live here when
they live anywhere. Magneto has been both the school's greatest threat and, briefly, its
headmaster.

**Current state [current].** Standing and quiet. Not yet loaded; Phase 2. Referenced in
dialogue only until then (Captain America knows the address; Spider-Man has been once).

**Who frequents it.** Xavier, the X-Men, students, visiting Avengers, Magneto on a bad day.

**Landmarks for the tilemap (Phase 2).** Long drive and iron gates with the school's name,
a stone mansion with ivy, basketball court that opens onto the Blackbird hangar, the lake
and boathouse, the woods, the cemetery for fallen X-Men. Interior: the war room, Cerebro's
chamber, the Danger Room (a training arena with configurable hazards), classrooms,
Xavier's study.

**Arc hooks.** Wolverine's and Storm's entrances to the roster; mutant-related street
incidents in Manhattan that lead north; Magneto as a Phase 2 world-tier antagonist.

## wakanda — Wakanda (Phase 2)

**Realm/region:** East-central Africa. The capital is Birnin Zana, the Golden City.

**History [fixed].** The most technologically advanced nation on Earth, built on the Great
Mound, where a vibranium meteorite struck in prehistory. Bashenga, the first Black Panther,
founded the Panther Cult and united the tribes; the heart-shaped herb grants the Panther
enhanced senses and strength. Wakanda was never colonized, hid its wealth behind a
pretense of poverty for centuries, and emerged on the world stage under King T'Chaka, who
was murdered by Ulysses Klaw during a vibranium raid (Fantastic Four #52-53). His son
T'Challa took the throne and the Panther mantle, joined the Avengers, and has defended the
Mound against Klaw, Doom, Namor and the entire world. The Dora Milaje guard the throne.
Shuri, T'Challa's sister, has worn the mantle herself.

**Current state [current].** T'Challa rules. Wakanda's borders are closed to the launch
roster except by invitation; the Wakandan embassy in New York is a dialogue location in
Phase 2. Not yet loaded.

**Who frequents it.** T'Challa, Shuri, Queen Mother Ramonda, the Dora Milaje, Wakandan
scientists, Avengers by invitation, Klaw and Doom without one.

**Landmarks for the tilemap (Phase 2).** Birnin Zana's skyline of towers with traditional
forms, the Great Mound (vibranium glow in the palette), the royal palace and throne room,
the Dora Milaje training grounds, the Necropolis, the Warrior Falls arena (ritual combat
set-piece), the Techno-Organic Jungle at the border, the embassy in Manhattan.

**Arc hooks.** Black Panther's roster entry; a vibranium theft in Manhattan traced to
Klaw or Doom; Wakanda's faction standing as a diplomatic resource; a hero's standing with
Wakanda opening the border.
